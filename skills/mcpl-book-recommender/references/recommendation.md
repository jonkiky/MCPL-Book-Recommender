# Recommendation rubric and output

## Ranking

Use qualitative judgments or the following heuristic internally; do not present scores as calibrated probabilities.

| Factor | Weight |
| --- | ---: |
| Interest, topic and reading goal | 35 |
| Genre, audience, style and format fit | 25 |
| Knowledge/reading difficulty or prerequisites | 15 |
| Credible recommendation evidence | 20 |
| Discovery value and variety | 5 |

Exclude explicit mismatches before ranking. Unknown evidence is not proof of low quality; seek verification or label as an unverified lead. A strong single-source recommendation can outrank a popular multi-source pick. Professional reviews and relevant expert/librarian recommendations generally carry more weight than sales rankings or isolated reader comments. For an explicit award/new-release request, use eligibility and date as filters. For learning, favor accurate, current editions and prerequisites; separate introductory from advanced books. Do not fold library stock into quality; availability is a separate constraint.

## Audience and intent

- General adults: match genre, mood, subject, reading time, complexity and preferred format; do not assume a particular genre.
- Nonfiction/technical learning: verify author expertise and edition currency; explain learning sequence and prerequisites. Distinguish evidence-based information from opinion.
- Book clubs: consider discussion potential, themes, accessibility and variety; provide discussion hooks when requested.
- Teens/YA: verify target audience and relevant content notes. Do not assume all YA fits every teen.
- Ages 0-3: board books, repetition and shared reading; 3-5: picture books and read-alouds; 5-7: early readers and beginning chapter books; 7-9: early chapters, comics and illustrated nonfiction; 9-12: middle grade; 12-14: older middle grade or suitable YA. These are discovery guides, not reading assessments. Distinguish independent reading from listening comprehension. Use verified reading levels or qualitative labels; never infer Lexile from age.

## Browse output

Start with the intent and assumptions. For each book give title/author, verified year, format/genre, concise hook, personalized reason, direct supporting evidence link(s), and borrowing link/status if checked. Preserve the original title and use the reader's language for explanations. For children include age guidance and reading mode/difficulty; for technical books include edition and prerequisites when relevant. Label availability as available, checked out, held/requestable if explicitly observed, in catalog/status unknown, not found in searched catalog, or unverified. Include branch and verification time only when checked. Present a short best-first pick or ordered learning path, and list accessed and unavailable sources. If evidence is thin, return fewer verified titles and separately label any provisional leads.

## Acceptance cases

- Adult open browsing: provide genre variety and independent evidence without child-only filtering.
- AI learning: recommend current editions and order by prerequisites, not by popularity.
- Read-alike: explain matching themes/tone; exclude the seed title and already-read titles.
- New releases: verify date and list freshness; distinguish sales rank from recommendation quality.
- Child reader: apply age/format constraints and never fabricate Lexile.
- Unavailable MCPL: keep best books and mark borrowing unknown; do not invent branch status.
- Available-now mode: include only verified available copies in the main list; label unknowns separately.
- No API keys: use public curated web sources and disclose inaccessible services.
- No network: label suggestions provisional; do not claim current rankings or availability.
